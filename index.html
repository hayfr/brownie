Welcome to The Brownie Bite

Our Purpose: To celebrate, educate, and share the joy of the ultimate baked good. Whether you love them fudgy, cakey, or loaded with nuts, this site is dedicated to honoring the rich history and undeniable magic of the chocolate brownie.

The Origin Story

The brownie wasn't an accident; it was a carefully crafted masterpiece. In 1893, socialite Bertha Palmer asked the pastry chef at the Palmer House Hotel in Chicago to create a dessert suitable for women attending the Chicago World's Columbian Exposition.


She requested a cake-like confection smaller than a piece of cake that could be easily eaten from boxed lunches. The result? The Palmer House Brownie, made with a decadent amount of chocolate, walnuts, and an apricot glaze. Remarkably, the hotel still serves the original recipe to this day!

Expert Research: Fun Facts & Statistics

  • The 3,000-Pound Behemoth: In 2001, an artisanal bakery in the Hudson Valley, New York, created the largest brownie on record. It weighed 3,000 pounds and was made with 750 lbs of chocolate chips and 500 lbs of butter.
  • The Name's Origin: The word "brownie" originally referenced mythical elf-like creatures found in Scottish folklore, made famous in the US by author Palmer Cox in the late 1800s. Early "brownie" recipes were actually molasses cakes; the chocolate variety didn't appear in cookbooks until 1904.
  • Consumption Statistics: It is estimated that Americans consume over 1.5 billion brownies every year. Furthermore, a massive culinary debate divides consumers: 52% of people prefer "fudgy" brownies, while around 24% prefer "cakey" (the rest fall into the chewy or un-opinionated categories).
